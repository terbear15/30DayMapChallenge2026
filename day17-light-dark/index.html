HOW TO READ THE LIGHT

Real imagery.
A moving solar clock.

Watch the same instant unfold across the contiguous United States. Sunlight is calculated for every location; time zones set the clocks.

City lights come from NASA’s 2016 Black Marble composite. Their gradual appearance is a solar-driven visualization, rather than hourly observations of lights switching on and off. Local behavior and cloud cover can differ.

The daytime surface is NASA’s November 2004 Blue Marble. Choosing a new date changes sunlight, while the satellite surface remains the same.

The four clocks follow the main Eastern, Central, Mountain, and Pacific time rules, including daylight saving. Arizona and other exceptions follow their own local rules. Optional boundary lines use U.S. Department of Transportation time-zone data, not the moving sunlight boundary.

Region details use a representative point to illustrate sunrise and sunset; those times vary across each region. Atmospheric refraction is included in sunrise and sunset calculations, while shading uses geometric solar altitude.

Cloud patterns are a static composite. Cloud and ocean reflectivity textures adapted from Solar System Scope under CC BY 4.0. Ocean relief uses NASA Blue Marble bathymetry.

Worldwide detail uses NASA GIBS Blue Marble Next Generation daytime tiles and the VIIRS 2016 Black Marble night composite. Both use the same 500 m tile pyramid across continents.
